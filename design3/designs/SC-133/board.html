What every option shares

Batches stays

SC-130's Batches and each batch's page (What happened, Money, Papers) are unchanged; every option leads to them.

Only the lines a batch has

A batch shows only the lines its plan has: no lot or truck without an ExpireSoon line, no van round without a scheme. The map is the distributor's own cluster (Hyderabad for Lakshmi).

Orders with the past

Every batch since July, each order with who bought, how many, at what price, and the paper that carries it (his tax invoice, issued or drafted; the food bank's receipt). Rakesh: ₹1,27,474 across 7 batches.

A · A card for each batch

Recommended

Today is one card for each batch in a journey: the batch named, its next step for him with its one button (the label photo, the buyer's truck, the van round, the staff sale, the invoice), and its lines (the scheme, the lot, the staff sale, the pickup) each with where it stands. Deliveries replaces the Van route: the batch's van round on its own cluster's map, the buyer's truck, the staff sale and the food bank's pickup, only the lines its plan has. Orders is every batch's orders, in a journey and cleared, grouped under the batch: who bought what, for how much, on which paper.

Why

  • The batch is all that is tracked: everything he does is under its batch, and the batch is named on every card.
  • No stub: a batch shows only the lines its plan has, so the Mango Drink has no Raipur lot, and its van round is drawn on Lakshmi's own Hyderabad cluster.
  • Orders explain themselves: each order says who bought, how many, at what price, and which paper carries it; the past is there by batch.
  • Batches stays as it is (SC-130), and each card leads to its page.

What it costs

  • Two batches in a journey make two cards; a busy distributor scrolls.
  • The van round and the truck are a tap deeper than today (from the card, or Deliveries).

What the build touches

  • design3 trade.jsx: DistHome, VanRoute (per batch), DistOrders; roles.jsx navigation.
  • core's trade screens, from the live case and the partner history (SC-130's partners.ts).
  • backend-api: nothing new for Today and Deliveries; Orders reads the partner route's facts.

B · One job list

Jobs replaces Today: what the batches ask of him now, each naming its batch, with its button; then what waits on others (the scheme filling, the lot's buyer, the food bank); then what is done today. Rounds replaces the Van route: every van round, truck and pickup, dated, coming then earlier, each with its batch, opening on its map and button. Orders is one ledger of every order, past and present, filterable by batch: date, batch, channel, who, packs, amount, paper.

Why

  • One place for everything to do, across all his batches.
  • A dated schedule of what leaves his godown, which a godown manager plans by.
  • The ledger answers 'what sold, when' in one look.

What it costs

  • The batch becomes a tag on each row rather than the frame: it is the job, not the batch, that leads.
  • A table on a phone becomes cards; the ledger reads best on a laptop.

What the build touches

  • The same screens as A, laid out as lists; a filter on Orders.

C · A batch in focus

Today, Deliveries and Orders each open on the batch in focus, picked from the batches in a journey at the top of the page (Orders adds 'All batches, past too'). Today is the batch's run sheet, every step from the Watcher's flag to settled, his own steps marked and actionable in place, beside its lines and how he ends whole on the plan. Deliveries and Orders are that batch's.

Why

  • Everything about one batch together: what happened, what is his, what left, what sold.
  • The run sheet shows where he is in the journey, not only what is next.

What it costs

  • Switching batches is a step; two batches in a journey are never side by side.
  • It overlaps the batch's own page on Batches (What happened, Money).
