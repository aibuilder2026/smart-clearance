What the papers show today

The GST numbers are not valid

None passes a GSTIN's check character: Munchly's 27AABCM1234F1Z5, Rakesh Traders' 27AABCR1234F1Z5, Agrawal Wholesale's 22AAGFA4821M1Z3. Lakshmi Agencies, Patil Distributors and Gupta & Sons have none, so a history invoice with Lakshmi Agencies as seller prints “GSTIN undefined”.

Munchly's own papers lack it

backend-api passes Paperwork the client's name only, so the PDFs Munchly issues (the credit notes, the ITC memo) never carry Munchly's GSTIN, PAN or address.

The invoice misses what rule 46 asks

No seller's address with its state and code, no place of supply, no reverse-charge line, no HSN summary, no amount in words, no signatory. The credit notes name no invoice they are against.

Valid, and fictional

Each GSTIN is built as the GST portal builds one: the state code, the PAN, the entity number, Z and its check character, which every one passes. Every PAN's four digits are 0000, which the Income Tax Department never issues, so none is a real taxpayer's. Each party also gets an address, an FSSAI licence where it trades in food, and Munchly a CIN.

Every paper of every batch

The tax invoices, the price-support and expiry credit notes, the GST ITC memo, the destruction certificate and the receipts, for the 12 batches cleared since July and the two in the journey, in the app's sheet and in the PDF Paperwork lays out, then every PDF laid out again.

The same figures

Every figure stays money.js's: the chips' INV/26-27/0931, 772 × ₹14.20, taxable ₹10,962.40, IGST 5% ₹548, round off −₹0.40, ₹11,510; CN/0117 ₹8,768. Only what the paper says about them is added. A price-support credit note stays a financial note, not under section 34, so no GST is adjusted, and it says so.

The options

Each shows the story's tax invoice, Rakesh Traders to Agrawal Wholesale (Maharashtra to Chhattisgarh, so IGST), and the price-support credit note, Munchly to Rakesh Traders.

Option A · As Tally prints it

The invoice is drafted for Rakesh to issue from Tally, so it reads as Tally's tax invoice: the seller's block, the invoice number and dates, delivery and dispatch, consignee and buyer with their GSTINs, state codes and the place of supply, the goods with HSN, quantity, rate and per, the HSN summary with the integrated tax, the amounts in words, the PAN, the bank and the declaration, “for Rakesh Traders · Authorised Signatory”. The credit note follows the same layout from Munchly.

Trade-offs
  • The layout every Indian distributor and accountant knows on sight.
  • Every field rule 46 asks for, and Munchly's own identity on its notes.
  • No e-invoice: a business turning over more than ₹5 crore must report a B2B invoice to the Invoice Registration Portal.

Option B · The e-invoice

Recommended

Option A, with what a distributor of Rakesh Traders' size has on a B2B invoice: the IRN, the acknowledgement number and date, and the signed QR code from the Invoice Registration Portal at the head. The credit note is as in A, and says why it is not reported to the portal (a financial note).

Trade-offs
  • The most realistic: e-invoicing has applied to every business over ₹5 crore of turnover since August 2023.
  • The IRN is worked out as the portal does (a SHA-256 of the GSTIN, year, type and number); the QR scans to its fields. Its signature is illustrative, and says so.
  • One more thing to explain in a demo.

Option C · The app's paper, completed

The paper keeps the app's own look, as today, with every missing field added: the seller's address, GSTIN, PAN and FSSAI licence, the buyer's GSTIN, the place of supply and reverse charge, HSN with the quantity and rate, the amount in words, the e-way bill line, the bank and the signatory.
