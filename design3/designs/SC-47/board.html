Quick-commerce gates per SKU, with a per-batch override

SC-47 · a client's Supply chain tab in the console · three options, built on the console's own shell, components and stylesheets, on Munchly Foods' SKUs and batches.

The maintainer's words, 6 Oct 2026:

Blinkit and Zepto/Instamart configurations should be at the SKU and batch level. This will later be used by the agents when they run for clients.

And the two answers that shaped it: per SKU, with a per-batch override, and the client's own values kept as the default for new SKUs.

Today a client has one pair of gates for everything it makes: Blinkit takes stock with at least 90 days of shelf life left, and Zepto and Instamart with at least 60% of it. A 180-day mango drink and a 730-day face wash are judged alike, and a lot a warehouse agreed to take on different terms can't say so.

The Supply chain tab now

What every option shares

One rule for the agents

  • A batch's gates are its override, else its SKU's, else the client's default, each of the two values on its own.
  • In the database: nullable gate_blinkit_days and gate_qcom_pct on skus (empty means the default) and on batches (the override), with its reason, who set it and when. Batches gain best_before, so days left is a fact rather than a note.
  • A view, sc.batch_gates, gives the agents each open batch's gates, where each came from, and pass or fail at Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart. The Watcher reads it.

The client's values become the default

  • The profile row reads Quick-commerce gates, by default: "New SKUs start at Blinkit 90+ days, Zepto and Instamart 60% of life · 3 of 8 SKUs differ".
  • In the profile sheet the two fields say they are for new SKUs. Changing them moves every SKU still on the default, and none with its own.
  • An SKU's values keep the profile's bounds (Blinkit 30 to 180 days, Zepto and Instamart 30 to 90%). An override may go down to 7 days and 5%, since it records a deal a warehouse agreed to, and it needs a reason.

Every change on the record

  • Audit lines in the signed-in person's name: "Set Mango Drink 200 ml's quick-commerce gates: Blinkit 45+ days, Zepto and Instamart 50% of life", "Overrode MF-2409-204's quick-commerce gates: Zepto and Instamart 35% (Zepto's Pune warehouse agreed…)", "Removed MF-2409-204's gate override".
  • An override holds until its batch closes, and a closed batch keeps the gates it was judged by.
  • Pass and fail always carry a tick or a cross and the figures, never colour alone.

A · On each SKU

Recommended

The SKU table gains each SKU's two gates, whether they are its own or the default, and its open batches. A row opens the SKU: its gates, and its batches with their pass or fail and any override.

Why
The gates sit where the SKUs already are, and a batch's override sits with the other batches of that SKU, so the whole story of one product is on one sheet. No new tab, and the sheet is the one the console already uses.
Trade-off
Changing many SKUs at once means opening each. Batches show only inside their SKU, so the tab has no list of every override.
What the build touches
  • backend-api: migration 0002 (SKU and batch gate columns, best_before, the view), the SKU and override routes, audit lines, hydrate and the Munchly import, tests.
  • design3: console.jsx's SupplyTab, an SkuSheet, ProfileSheet's labels, platform.js.
  • frontend: @smart-clearance/api (types, HTTP, mock), the console's Supply chain tab and a new SkuSheet.

B · A gates matrix

A section of its own: the default and every SKU in one table you edit in place, saved together; under it, every batch override, and a sheet to add one.

Why
The fastest way to set up a new client's range: every SKU on one screen, typed straight in, saved once. Every override in the workspace is listed in one place.
Trade-off
A second table of the same SKUs beside the SKU table, and edit-in-place needs a save bar and leave-without-saving care. The batch picker grows long for a large range.
What the build touches
As A, with a GatesMatrix (inline fields and a save bar) and an OverrideSheet in place of the SkuSheet.

C · Batch first

A Batches tab shows every open batch against the gates the Watcher reads: days left, Blinkit, and Zepto and Instamart, each with where the value came from. A batch opens to its gates and its override.

Why
It shows exactly what the agents will see: every batch, pass or fail, and why. Staff can spot a lot that a gate is blocking and override it where they see it.
Trade-off
A new tab, and gates are edited in two places: an SKU's on Supply chain, a batch's here. The tab duplicates part of the workspace's own Batches screen.
What the build touches
As A, plus a client batches route and a Batches tab with its BatchSheet; the SKU table gains the two columns, edited in a small sheet.
Open option CA batch opened

Recommended: A. Gates belong to products, and A keeps them on the SKU, with each batch's exception beside its siblings. It adds no tab and reuses the sheet staff already know. B is the better tool for loading a large range in one sitting, and its matrix could come later as a bulk edit. C's view of every batch is what the agents' own screens will show, so it is better built with them than here.
