In every option

  • The heading, subtitle and both buttons stay as they are.
  • The nine stops play over the town plate. Each stop's agent works in turn, with a beat of about a second on the person's yes. A caption under the carton says what each one did: "4 of 9 · Value · Valuer · five exits priced · the bin costs ₹26,330".
  • It plays once, as soon as the plate has loaded, in 4.7 seconds. It holds on "Sold, not binned. ₹21,152 recovered, instead of −₹26,330 to destroy it" and offers Replay. Under reduced motion the result shows at once, and nothing loops (WCAG 2.2.2).
  • "Nine stops. Ten agents. One yes." goes, and the nav's Agents link lands on the hero. The batch card on the plate goes too, since the caption carries the stop and the money.
  • No client: "a person" says yes, and the batch is the illustrative one, every figure from core/money.js.
  • Screen readers get the nine stops as a list; the drawing is hidden from them.

ThreeUI

  • The MCP server isn't installed. Claude Code's auto mode refused to add an MCP server to your user settings on my own, and ThreeUI's MCP is for Pro members only (it signs in with OAuth). The command to add it yourself is in decision.md.
  • Options 1 and 2 adapt two free Community effects: the constellation field and the gateway flow, from @designcodeio/threeui 1.2.0 (MIT, © 2026 Meng To; the licence is in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
  • Redrawn in design system v3: the system's inks over the town plate, without the effects' soft halos (the aura and the scanline stay the only glows), and stopped within five seconds instead of looping.
  • Option 3 uses no ThreeUI code. Its threads borrow the look of ThreeUI's interface lines.

Checked: every mockup was scanned with axe (the suite's WCAG 2.2 AA tags and its target-size pass) in light and dark at 1440 and 390, both mid-walk at the person's yes and at rest. There were zero violations. Every piece of text drawn over the plate was also measured against the pixels behind it: all 260 pass 4.5:1, and the lowest is 4.70:1.

The hero now, and the section that merges into it

Option 1 · The agents' constellation

The agents become a constellation over the carton, and the batch's handoffs draw it

Recommended

The ten agents and the person are named stars on an arc over the giant carton, in a faint field of linked nodes (ThreeUI's constellation field). As the batch moves:

  • each stop's star lights, and the caption under the carton says what its agent did;
  • a green line draws from star to star as each agent hands off, amber through the person's yes;
  • at rest the whole arc is lit: ten agents, one person, one batch sold;
  • the field leans toward the pointer for a moment when it moves (ThreeUI's gravity), then settles.
Open the mockupIn dark
Why recommended
It reads at a glance as a team of AI agents working your stock: the agentic part you asked to see first. The carton and the town stay the picture: the arc sits around the top of the carton, under the buttons, so it hides little of the town. On desktops every name is on screen at once.
Trade-off
It has the most "network" look of the three. It is kept to fine ink lines with no glow, so it doesn't tip into a neon AI dashboard. On phones only the agent at work is named; the rest are stars.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: the hero's overlay (a canvas for the field, the named stars, the caption and Replay); the stops section removed; the batch card retired;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same, with motion and a canvas;
  • ThreeUI's MIT notice, shipped with it.

Option 2 · Through the gateway

The packs stream out of the carton to one gate, where the agents work and the person says yes

Dotted paths run from the carton to a gate beside it (ThreeUI's gateway flow). The packs flow along them, and they:

  • gather at the gate while each agent works there in turn, its icon and name on the gate;
  • wait for the person's yes, when the gate turns amber, then green;
  • leave for their exits, green to the kiranas and violet to the marketplace buyer, each exit counting them in to 588 and 772 packs.

The staff sale, the food bank and the bin stay dashed: priced, and not needed.

Open the mockupIn dark
Strength
The most literal story of stock being diverted: you see where the packs go, and the counts land.
Trade-off
The agents appear one at a time at the gate, so "ten agents" is told by the caption more than seen. It also previews the street section further down the page. On phones the exits sit in a list under the picture.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: the hero's overlay (the paths and packs on a canvas, the gate, the exits and their counters, the caption and Replay); the stops section removed; the batch card retired;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same;
  • ThreeUI's MIT notice, shipped with it.

Option 3 · The carton's crew

The agents ride a ring around the carton, and each comes to the front to do its part

The ten agents and the person sit on a tilted ring around the giant carton, passing behind it. The ring turns once:

  • each stop's agent comes to the front and wears the aura while it works, and the caption says what it did;
  • a thread draws from each agent to the next, so the crew's work leaves a web around the carton;
  • it waits a beat on the person's yes, then rests with every agent lit.
Open the mockupIn dark
Strength
The most surreal and three-dimensional of the three: the agents physically surround the stock.
Trade-off
The busiest motion: the ring moves on ten times in under five seconds. Names at the back are smaller, and at any moment one or two agents are behind the carton. On phones the tiles are icons, with only the agent at work named.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: the hero's overlay (the ring, its tiles clipped behind the carton, the threads, the caption and Replay); the stops section removed; the batch card retired;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same, with motion.

Decide

Pick one option for the hero, or ask for another round. Parts can move between options before the build: option 2's exits beside option 1's constellation, for example.

The pick is built in two places, as SC-28 was: first the prototype in design3/site, then the SvelteKit port. The drift, parity and accessibility suites hold the port to the prototype.
