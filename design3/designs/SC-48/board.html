The console's Overview as a live dashboard

SC-48 · console.smartclearance.com · three options, built on the console's own shell, components and stylesheets. Every figure on them is illustrative; the build reads each one from backend-api.

The maintainer's words, 6 Oct 2026:

The overview page for the console can be made more intuitive with dashboards and live charts, and proper pagination of the live batches running. Think of a proper, engaging dashboard experience, then implement it. Make it dynamic and database-driven: no hard-coding or stubbing in the UI.

Overview today is a list of tracker cards, one per batch, with no totals, no trend and no paging. With backend-api (SC-45) and its synthetic world it shows 9 clients and 31 batches, and a real workspace would bring hundreds.

The Overview now

What every option shares

Read from the database

  • Two new routes in backend-api: GET /v1/console/dashboard (the figures, the series, the stops, Needs attention) and GET /v1/console/batches, paginated on the server, with status, client, stop, q, sort, page and size.
  • Every figure is a SQL aggregate over batches, agent_runs and clients; nothing is written into the page.
  • The in-browser mock answers the same shapes from platform.js, and hydrate writes 30 days of history (opened and closed batches, units, what each recovered) so the charts have a past.

Live, and still

  • The figures are read again every 30 seconds, and when the window comes back into focus. The page says when it last read them.
  • Pause updates stops the reading (WCAG 2.2.2 covers auto-updating content), and the choice is remembered.
  • Nothing moves on its own: no rolling numbers or pulsing dots. A new reading swaps the figures in place.

Charts and the table

  • The dataviz rules: one series in the primary green, with amber kept for waiting for a person, which always carries its label. Lines are 2 px; bars are 24 px or thinner, with 4 px rounded ends.
  • Every chart has a hover tooltip, a one-line summary for screen readers, and Show as a table.
  • The batch table has filters in one row (search, client, stop, status) and 8, 16 or 32 rows a page. Desktops page by number; phones page with previous and next. The page and the filters live in the address, so a refresh or a shared link keeps them.

A · Command centre

Recommended

Four figures with their 30-day lines, then recovered by day beside the batches in flight at each stop. Choosing a stop filters the live table below it; the lists sit at the foot.

Why
The question "how are we doing?" is answered in the first screen: money, volume, the queue waiting on people, and the agents' pace, each with its trend. The stops chart is both a picture and a filter, so the table is one click from any count.
Trade-off
The most to build: four series, two charts and the table, each with its tests. On a laptop the table starts below the fold.
What the build touches
  • backend-api: the two routes, their SQL, hydrate's history, contract tests.
  • design3: console/console.jsx's Overview, console.css, and the mock's aggregates in core/platform.js.
  • frontend: @smart-clearance/api (types, HTTP, mock); core gains Sparkline, AreaChart, StopBars and Pager; the console's Overview screen, with TanStack Query refetching.

B · The nine stops

The pipeline leads: every stop with its count and its agents, Approve in amber. Choosing a stop lists its batches underneath; the charts and the lists come after.

Why
It teaches the product: the nine stops are the story every client is sold, and the band shows where each batch stands and which agent has it.
Trade-off
The trend comes last, and a stop is always chosen, so the table never lists every batch at once without a click. The band needs its own keyboard and phone layouts.
What the build touches
As A, with a StopBand in place of the stops chart and one trend chart fewer at the top.

C · Clients at a glance

A card per client: what it recovered in 30 days with its line, its batches at each stop, and what waits on it. Choosing a card lists that client's batches.

Why
Staff work client by client: who is doing well, who is stuck setting up, whose batches wait on a person. It scales to a few dozen clients before it needs a search.
Trade-off
Platform-wide totals get one row of tiles and no chart of their own, and with many clients the cards push the table far down.
What the build touches
As A, with per-client series in the dashboard route, and a ClientCard in place of the two charts.
Open option CA client chosen

Recommended: A. It answers how the platform is doing before anything is clicked, keeps every batch one filter away, and its stops chart doubles as the way into the table. B's band is a good teaching picture, but it makes staff choose a stop before they see a list. C is the right shape once there are many clients, and its card could later sit on the Clients page.
