The landing page, refined

Three ways to bring smartclearance.com to the SC-25 board's sections and fonts, without any client on the page, and to show what Smart-Clearance does with motion: the agents at work, the money on screen, the one yes.

The request

"I want platform landing page section designs to match this [the SC-25 board], including the fonts.... The screenshot I pasted looks better for the 'Your own workspace' section. Dont include details of Munchly on landing page as they are our client, See landing page of spoileralert.com and nearxpiry.com. Rest all look fine. Design has to be professional and engaging."
"[Five exits, one batch] This looks good, the picture, but may be also something more engaging so that users understands what smart clearance is about."
"Agentic flows can be shown with some motion animations which look cool.... Think of images and quick video clips may be.... or motion animations to depict the business."

In every option

  • The board's sections and fonts.
    • The street and the nine stops keep the condensed display heading.
    • Teams, plans and the close take the normal-width heading the comps show.
    • The street gains the board's three result cards (L2), each still carrying its arithmetic.
  • "Your own workspace" as comp L5: islands with products and an address under each, four team cards, and the integration chips.
  • No client details.
    • Gone: the client's name, packs, people, workspace address, batch ids, story section, and the sign-in menu entry.
    • The figures stay, labelled as an illustrative batch.
  • Motion plays once when it comes into view, in under five seconds, holds on the result and offers Replay; under reduced motion it arrives in place.

From the references

  • Spoiler Alert leads with one plain promise, then three phases, team benefits, results and an intro call.
  • NearXpiry answers "How do I sell? How do I buy?" in three steps, then why, numbers, reviews and questions.
  • Both explain the mechanism before they ask for anything. The options borrow that order, never their claims: every figure here is the illustrative batch's, worked out from the journey map.

The page now

What leaves in every option:

  • the batch id MF-2409-117;
  • "Inside Munchly's workspace…";
  • "Customers: Munchly Foods" in the footer and in the sign-in menu.

Option A · The board, explained and moving

The board's page, with one new section and the agents at work

Recommended

The board's sections in the board's fonts, plus one short new section under the hero, "From at risk to sold, in three steps", that says what Smart-Clearance does before the street shows it. The nine stops become a live pipeline: the batch walks the rail, each agent lights up and says what it did, and the flow pauses on the human yes in amber.

Open the mockupIn dark
Why recommended
It changes the least of what you said looks fine and matches the board most closely, and it answers both of your notes where they arose: "what Smart-Clearance is about", above the street, and the agentic motion, in the nine stops.
Trade-off
A step card is a familiar pattern. The renders and the agents lighting in turn keep the cards from feeling stock.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: a new steps section, the stops as a pipeline, the result cards, workspace L5, plans and close L6, and the client removed;
  • the SvelteKit port: the same sections, plus the pipeline in motion;
  • new renders in design3/system/img.

Option B · Explained like a platform

The references' order: how it works, the proof, the teams, the questions

"How Smart-Clearance works" is told in three product moments, each the card a team actually sees:

  • the Watcher's 09:00 alert, with the quick-commerce gates failing;
  • the Valuer's prices for every exit, and the Router's split;
  • the plan waiting for one yes, with the agents waking as it is approved.

Then come the street with its results, the nine stops, your workspace, and a short questions section.

Open the mockupIn dark
Strength
The clearest answer to "what is it about": a visitor sees the actual product, not a description of it. It is closest to how Spoiler Alert sells.
Trade-off
It is the longest page, and it moves furthest from the board. The nine stops become a static summary because the moments carry the motion.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: three moment cards built from the kit (gate chips, the Valuer's rows, the approve sheet), a questions section, and the sections shared with option A;
  • the port, the same.

Option C · The town at work

The miniature world in motion: short clips, played once

The diorama tells the story:

  • The hero: the town comes alive. Tiny people stroll, the van moves off and the green path brightens, while the view drifts slowly over the town.
  • The street: its five exits are priced one after another as it scrolls in.
  • The nine stops: in the square's clip the amber button glows, then the vans drive off across the square, while a rail of the nine stops lights beneath it, the yes in amber.

Each clip is about five seconds long, plays once and holds on its last frame.

Open the mockupIn dark
Strength
The most memorable, and the most in the page's own world: the town itself does the explaining.
Trade-off
It explains the least in words, and the clips add 1 to 2 MB to the page. These two are drafts: LTX moved the camera in both, though asked not to, and the tap on the phone doesn't read. The build would make them again with the last frame pinned to the plate, which holds the camera, and make night versions too. Until then, dark mode shows the night plates as stills.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: video in the hero and the stops, with posters, Play again, and the last frame under reduced motion; the street's chips priced in turn; and the sections shared with option A;
  • the clips in design3/site/assets/media;
  • the port, the same.

Decide

Pick one option, or ask for another round. Parts can move between options before the build: option C's town clip in option A's hero, say, or option B's questions under option A.

Once you pick, the build happens in two places. First the prototype in design3/site, which is the source of truth, re-pinned on Claude Design. Then the SvelteKit port, held to it by the drift, parity and accessibility suites.
