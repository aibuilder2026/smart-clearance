The maintainer (12 Oct), with a screenshot of the console's sign-in on an iPad in portrait:

“on mobile and ipad, the login graph does not great in portrait mode, need better design just for these devices, rest all is fine”

Picked C, built

The pick (12 Oct): C, Around the card, and tablets held sideways below 1100 px take it too. Built in the kit's AgentStage below desktops, design3 first, then the SvelteKit port, for both sign-ins.

Round the card

The field fills the sign-in behind the card, pinned as a long card scrolls. The eleven stand on a loop below the pill, round the card's edges; a tap on a tile holds the walk on that agent, a tap on the field sends a ripple and lets go.

The pill

Who is at work and what they did, Pause, and the nine stops as a rail: each a button you can tap, or reach by keyboard (one stop in a tab), to hold the walk on that stop's agent.

Desktops unchanged

SC-195's stage beside the card. On a phone the card takes its own glass over the field; under reduced motion every agent is drawn lit.

Today

The desktop's wide orbit is squeezed into a short, wide band: its nodes read as faint scribbles, and only the agent at work shows. On a tall tablet the band also stretches with the sign-in's rows, leaving an empty slab under its caption. That stretch is fixed in all three options.

The same agents and walk

The ten agents and you, in the order they work the nine stops; the loop, Pause and Play, and reduced motion as built in SC-195. Only the layout below desktops changes.

Every agent at hand

In all three, each agent is a tile you can tap (or reach by keyboard, one stop in a tab) to hold the walk on it and read what it did; a tap on the field sends a ripple and lets go.

Light and dark

Tiles stay opaque over the field's lit nodes, names sit on their own ground so the threads never cross their words, and every ink is the design system's.

Option A · The ring

Recommended

The eleven leave the desktop's wide orbit for a ring: round a circle from the top, clockwise, every agent a tile you can see and tap, the field inside it, and the caption in its middle. The handoffs draw round the ring, the yes amber, the three exits fanning from it. On an iPad each tile carries its name, outward.

Trade-offs
  • The graph keeps its shape in portrait: a whole loop, not a flattened arc; every agent shows, not only the one at work.
  • The most like the desktop's stage, so the product reads the same on every screen.
  • The tallest of the three: on a phone the card starts about where it does today (≈330 px down); on an iPad the ring is 440 px.

Option B · The relay

The agents line up as the batch meets them: nine stops across the band, every agent a tile on one track, the three exits stacked at Execute where the yes fans out to them, meeting again at Paperwork. The handoff draws along the track. An iPad names each stop over the track and each agent under its tile.

Trade-offs
  • Reads like the product's own tracker: left to right, one stop after another.
  • The lowest: on a phone the card rises about 90 px.
  • Less of a picture: a diagram more than a mesh; on a phone the tiles are close (28 px, 37 px apart) and unnamed.

Option C · Around the card

The band goes. The field fills the whole screen behind the card, as on a desktop, and the eleven stand on a loop round the card's edges, the handoffs passing under its glass. A slim pill at the top says who is at work, with the nine stops as a rail, and Pause.

Trade-offs
  • The card sits highest, and the sign-in feels like the desktop's: one picture behind everything.
  • On an iPad the agents frame the card down both sides.
  • On a phone most of the loop is under the card: the pill tells the story, the field is atmosphere; the card takes its own glass to stay legible.

Recommended: A, The ring

It answers the complaint most directly: in portrait the graph becomes a whole, legible loop instead of a squashed arc, every agent visible and tappable, and it stays recognisably the desktop's stage. B is the compact choice if the card should rise; C is the quiet one, best on an iPad.
