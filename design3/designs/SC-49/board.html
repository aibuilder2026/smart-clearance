The console, in motion

SC-49 · console.smartclearance.com · three options, built on the console itself (its shell, components and stylesheets) with the motion each would ship. Every company, person and figure on them is fictional.

The maintainer's words, 6 Oct 2026:

On the console, I want some cool loader effects, like a hydrating effect when I change tabs. Pressing the Sign in button should be more engaging: at the moment, clicking Sign in removes the text. Make the console more engaging, especially the Overview's graphs and charts, and the way the agents are running in batches.

The console now

Sign in
The button's loading state makes its label and icon transparent and puts a spinner in their place, so for over a second it's an empty green bar.
Changing screens and tabs
In design3 the next screen fades in over 180 ms. In the build each screen's load waits for its reads from backend-api (prefetch), and meanwhile the old screen stays put, with nothing to show the click was heard.
The Overview
The figures and charts are drawn once. Each reading (every 30 s) swaps them in place, and the agents' work appears only as counts by stop and a list of runs.

What every option shares

A loader in the screen's shape

  • Placeholders take the shape of the screen on its way: the dashboard, a table, a list, cards, the agents' pipeline, a form, or a client page with its header and tabs.
  • The loader starts the moment a link or tab is pressed. In the build that's SvelteKit's navigating, so the old screen never sits there unchanged.
  • A screen's first tab arrives with it; a later tab loads on its own. Screen readers hear "Loading Overview" once.

Sign in keeps its label

  • "Signing in…" while it checks, then a confirmation before the console opens. The button can't be pressed twice.
  • Screen readers hear "Signing in", then "Signed in".
  • A wrong sign-in still shows SC-46's one message.

Live, pausable, still when asked

  • Each reading of the Overview (every 30 s in the build; every 6 s in the mockups so you can see it) moves only what it changed.
  • Pause updates stops all of it (WCAG 2.2.2). Nothing loops: only a loader moves, and only while something loads.
  • Under reduced motion every step lands at once. Every figure still comes from backend-api's dashboard and batches.

A · The route

Recommended

The landing page's loader, carried into the console. A green route draws along the top of whatever is loading and lands its amber pin, while the screen's shape waits underneath and hydrates in a green wash. Sign in draws the mark's S. On the Overview the figures roll and the charts draw themselves, and Agents at work shows every batch as its client's mark, travelling the nine stops as the agents finish.

Motion
  • Route: eases to 88% (about 1.5 s at most), then waits; when the data arrives it completes in 160 ms and the pin lands (420 ms).
  • Wash: one pass, 1.15 s, block after block (70 ms apart), only while loading. The content then rises 8 px into place, 50 ms apart.
  • Sign in: the S draws in 1.1 s, with a line along the foot. Then "Welcome, Neha" and a tick (spring 520/22/1). A wrong sign-in shakes for 360 ms.
  • Overview: figures roll (the kit's Roll, 700 ms); the line draws in 900 ms; a reading moves it in 420 ms and marks today's gain. Tokens travel on a spring (170/24/1), and the stop they reach lights for 1.6 s.
Why
It is Smart-Clearance's own motion: the loader's route and pin, now in the console's working rhythm. Agents at work answers "what are the agents doing?" at a glance, because each client's batches are seen moving stop to stop, and it carries the counts and the pick that "In flight, by stop" carried, so it takes that card's place.
Trade-off
The most to build. The route needs to know which batches sit at each stop and when each last moved, which the dashboard doesn't send yet. With many batches at one stop, only three marks show, plus a count.
What the build touches
  • backend-api: a migration adding batches.stage_at, written by the services when a batch moves; the dashboard gains each stop's latest three batches and today's closed batches; the contract, the mock and OpenAPI follow.
  • core: RouteBar, Hydrate (the shapes) and Roll, with the springs added to SPRINGS.
  • console: the loader around each screen and tab from navigating; the Sign in button; Kpi, Sparkline and RecoveredChart; a new AgentsRoute in place of StopBars.
  • design3: console.jsx and console.css first.

B · The fill

"Hydrating", taken literally. The screen's shape fills with green ink while it loads, and the content surfaces as it drains. Sign in fills as it checks, then floods the page green and clears onto the console. The figures count up, the charts fill from the baseline, and The flow shows each stop as a vessel: a drop falls to the next one as an agent finishes, and the levels settle.

Motion
  • Fill: each placeholder fills to 86% (about 1.5 s at most, 40 ms apart). When the data arrives it tops up, the content surfaces (320 ms) and the ink drains.
  • Sign in: ink fills the button in 1.3 s. Signed in, a circle of deep green floods the page from the button (1.2 s, over in 0.5 s) and clears onto the console. A wrong sign-in tilts the button for 420 ms.
  • Overview: figures count up (700 ms); areas fill from the baseline (1 s); a reading drops into today with a splash. Vessels settle on a spring with a little slosh (140/14/1), and drops arc in 800 ms.
Why
It is the most literal answer to "hydrating", and the most playful. The vessels read as how full each stop is, which suits a queue.
Trade-off
The flood is a full-screen change of colour on every sign-in, which wears with daily use. Liquid isn't part of the design system's world (the route, the godown, the miniature town). The vessels show how many batches, not whose.
What the build touches
As A, without the migration: the counts the dashboard sends already fill the vessels, and a drop follows each change in the counts; today's closed batches are added. Hydrate becomes Fill, the flood is a new overlay in App, and a new AgentsFlow takes the place of StopBars.

C · Quiet and quick

Motion only where it tells you something. A thin progress line and the kit's placeholders while a screen loads, then the content at once. Sign in turns its icon into a spinner beside its label. The charts sit still until a reading changes them; then that change moves and is marked for a moment. Live activity lists the agents' runs as they land and the batches they moved, beside the existing bars by stop.

Motion
  • Loading: the line eases to 80% and completes; the kit's shimmer runs only while loading; the content fades in over 160 ms.
  • Sign in: the kit's spinner in the icon's place, then "Signed in" for 320 ms.
  • Overview: a changed figure is marked for 1.8 s (a green ground and an arrow); lines and bars ease to new values in 420 ms; new runs slide in (240 ms) and are marked for 2.4 s; a moved batch fills its stop segments (420 ms).
Why
The calmest and the fastest to read: nothing moves unless something changed, which suits a screen staff keep open all day. It fixes Sign in with the smallest change.
Trade-off
It answers "more engaging" the least. There's no hydrating effect, just the kit's shimmer, and the agents appear as a feed, not as work moving through the stops.
What the build touches
The least in the interface. Core gains a Progress line; the console gets the button's label and spinner, the marks on changed figures, and a LiveActivity card. The feed reads today's runs as it does now. To list the batches that moved, the dashboard needs to know when each batch last moved, which is the same batches.stage_at migration as A's.

Recommended: A. It gives the console the hydrating loader that was asked for in Smart-Clearance's own language (the route and its pin, as on the landing page), fixes Sign in without losing its label, and turns the Overview's agents from counts into work you can watch. B is more playful, but its flood and liquid sit outside the design system's world. C is the safe floor, and its marks on changed figures could join A.

Open question. The mockups read every 6 seconds so the motion can be seen; the build reads every 30. With the changes now easy to follow, should the Overview read more often, say every 10 seconds?
