Today

The staff sale has no paper

Lakshmi Agencies records one number, the packs sold, and that is all the batch keeps. Nothing shows who paid, or how: the UPI code on the card is an illustration. Two of the pilot quarter's batches had a staff sale (the Poha's 150 packs at ₹22, the Oats' 50 at ₹26), and the Mango Drink has one now (150 at ₹8).

The certificate is thin

The agency's certificate carries the packs, the kilos, one method line, the site as “the municipal landfill, Nagpur”, one time and the GST. It has no reason, no facility address or location, no start time, no officer and no witnesses, and nobody signs it.

Nothing is signed

The tax invoices and credit notes print “for … / Authorised Signatory” over a blank, then “This is a Computer Generated Document”. The certificate, the ITC memo and the FSSAI checklist have no signature block at all.

Part 1 of 3

The staff sale's consolidated receipt

The Mango Drink at Lakshmi Agencies: 150 packs at ₹8, staff paying by UPI over two days. Every option yields one paper listing every payment by its UPI reference (the 12-digit RRN a payer's UPI app shows). In the mockups, 22 staff members paid ₹1,200 in all. The receipt becomes evidence in three places: the distributor's batch papers, Priya's pack and the batch's Record.

Option A · With the count

Recommended

The receipt is attached on the same card as the count. Vision reads it, and the sale records once the payments match.

  • Lakshmi Agencies: under the count, a slot for the consolidated receipt: a photo or PDF, from her books or her UPI app's statement. Vision reads each UPI reference, who paid and how much. “22 UPI payments · ₹1,200 · matches 150 × ₹8” turns Record on.
  • Short: if the payments come to less, the card says so and offers Send another, or Record 144 sold.
  • Priya: Execution's staff sale card shows the receipt and Vision's read. The pack gains “Staff sale receipt”: the photo she sent, with the references read from it.
Trade-offs
  • One step for the distributor, and the evidence can't be forgotten: the sale doesn't record without it.
  • The same pattern as the label photo and the destruction's photos.
  • A sale is recorded only once its last payment is in.

Option B · A step of its own

The count stays as it is today. The receipt follows as the distributor's next step, and the papers wait for it.

  • Lakshmi Agencies: records the count as today, then her card and her Today ask for the consolidated receipt, with a reminder after a day. Vision reads it once it comes.
  • Priya: the pack shows “Staff sale receipt · awaiting”, and Mark reviewed waits for it.
Trade-offs
  • The sale is recorded the moment it ends; the receipt can come a day later, once the statement is out.
  • One more thing to chase, and the papers' review can stall on it.

Option C · The payments are the count

She uploads her UPI app's statement. The staff payments in it make the count, and the Paperwork agent lays out the receipt.

  • Lakshmi Agencies: uploads the statement for the sale's days, as the CSV or PDF her UPI app exports. The Data agent lists the payments into her address. Those at ₹8 a pack come ticked; others, such as a shop's ₹1,450, come unticked. The count follows from the ticks.
  • The paper: a consolidated receipt voucher in Tally's layout, with every UPI reference, signed with her signature on file.
  • Priya: the voucher in the pack.
Trade-offs
  • No stepper to get wrong: the count is the money received.
  • Depends on a statement export, and on the payments' amounts: two staff paying together, or a part payment, takes a tick by hand.
  • The most to build (a statement parser, the voucher's PDF).
Part 2 of 3

The certificate of destruction

MF-2407-111, the Mango Drink at Rakesh Traders: 184 packs destroyed through Orange City Enviro Services, certificate OCE/DC/26-27/0217. Both options carry every mandatory item; they differ in how the paper is laid out.

In both

What it carries

  • The goods: brand, product, HSN, MRP, made and best-before dates, the batch number, exact packs (cartons and loose) and total weight, split into food and packaging.
  • Reason: product expiry (ticked), contamination or damage, court-ordered seizure.
  • Method: CPCB-compliant sanitary landfill or incineration, by the agency's own method.
  • Where and when: the facility's name, address and coordinates, the date, and the time from start to finish.
  • Signatures: the supervising officer, with the agency's seal, and two independent witnesses with their addresses; who handed the goods over; whose yes approved the evidence.

Where the facts come from

The agency's record, kept in the console's Rules, gains its facility, its coordinates, its method and its supervising officer. The distributor's destruction screen gains the time window and the two witnesses (below). The date and the end time come from the after photo's slate and time.

Past and present

The pilot quarter's three certificates are restated in full: DGW/DC/26-27/0085, OCE/DC/26-27/0217 and OCE/DC/26-27/0218. So is the slow week's OCE/DC/26-27/0219, along with every one the journey issues from now on. Deccan Green Waste Management, in Hyderabad, incinerates at its common facility; the Nagpur agencies landfill.

Option A · One page, numbered

Recommended

One A4 page in the agency's letterhead. Each mandatory item is a numbered section, and the three signatures sit at its foot.

Trade-offs
  • Each item of the audit trail sits under its own heading: an auditor can check it item by item.
  • The two photos stay on the batch's Record, not on the paper.

Option B · The certificate and its log

Page 1 is the certificate as a summary, with the signatures. Page 2 is the destruction log: each step's time, from the godown to the cover, with the weighbridge slip, the vehicle's seal and both photos. The three signatories initial every step.

Trade-offs
  • The fullest audit trail: the photos and every step are on the paper itself.
  • Two pages; the log's steps (loading, weighing, covering) are the agency's to record, so the demo invents their times around the photos' times.

Option A · Ink and the firm's stamp

Recommended

The signature in ink over the firm's rubber stamp, the way Indian traders sign and stamp a bill, with the signatory's name and title under “Authorised Signatory”.

  • Distributors: the rectangular “For RAKESH TRADERS · Proprietor” stamp.
  • Munchly and the agencies: a round seal.
  • “This is a Computer Generated Document” goes, since the paper is now signed.
Trade-offs
  • Reads at a glance as a signed paper, on screen and printed.
  • One look for every paper, the certificate's signatures included.

Option B · Digitally signed

The GST papers and credit notes carry a digital signature's box, as Tally prints with a Class 3 DSC: who signed, when, why and where. The certificate keeps its ink, since its signatories sign at the site.

Trade-offs
  • How larger distributors actually sign e-invoices.
  • Less like a signature at a glance; the validity tick is illustrative, as the e-invoice's QR already is.
  • Two looks: boxes on the GST papers, ink on the certificate.
