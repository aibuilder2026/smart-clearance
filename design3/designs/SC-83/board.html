A gated batch that sells through

SC-83 · Munchly's workspace · three options for the watchlist row of a batch that fails a quick-commerce gate but sells out in normal trade before retailers stop taking it. Every company, person and figure is fictional.

The maintainer (8 Oct), on the live Command Center:

  • “Some of them are showing gated selling through, but shows cross on Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart, so they should be at risk.”
  • Asked whether such a batch should count as at risk: keep the rule, with a clearer row.

The rule stays as money.js has it: a batch is at risk only when packs will not sell before the last week and every gate fails. Peanut Chikki (MF-2408-311): 960 packs, 61 days left, 20 a day: all 960 sell in 48 days, 6 before retailers stop, though quick commerce takes none of it.

The row now

Three red crosses and an amber badge on a batch that needs nothing; red is meant for risk and the bin.

What all three share

The rule stays

  • At risk, gated and safe are money.js's, unchanged; the Watcher flags what it flags today.
  • Only a batch that is gated and sells through changes; at-risk and safe rows are as they are.

Red means risk

  • A failed gate is red only on a batch at risk. On a batch in motion, cleared or selling through, it is neutral (B folds it into one chip).

From the data

  • The sell-out day and the margin come from the batch's packs, its sales a day and its best-before, as money.js projects them; nothing is typed.
  • Every screen that shows the row gets it: the Command Center, the Batches list on phones, a distributor's own stock.

Option A · Sells out by

Recommended

The row keeps its badge and gates, and gains the reason it is fine, on its own line under the bar: ✓ Sells out by 19 Nov · 6 days to spare, in the brand's green. A failed gate is drawn neutral unless the batch is at risk: red stays for risk and the bin.

Trade-offs
  • Says why in words, with the date and the margin, which is what was asked.
  • The gates are still there to read, and red keeps one meaning: a failed gate turns red only on a batch at risk (the at-risk hero, or a batch the Watcher flagged).
  • A gated row is a line taller than the others (96 px against 78 px); on phones, where the gates are hidden, the line is the only word on the gates.
What the build touches
The kit's BatchRow and GateChips (a neutral fail when not at risk), components.css, then core's two components. The sell-out day comes from the batch's packs, its sales a day and its best-before (money.js's projection), so no new figure is typed.

Option B · One chip

For a batch that sells through, the badge says Selling through in trade (neutral), the line says when it sells out, and the failed gates fold into one neutral chip that names them: Not for quick commerce, or Not for Zepto, Instamart when Blinkit passes.

Trade-offs
  • The quietest row: no crosses at all where nothing is wrong.
  • Rows keep one height.
  • It renames a status the Batches screen's filters, the Route Room and the design system call Gated, and hides which gate failed behind the chip's title.
What the build touches
As A, plus the status's label in the kit's STATUS (and core's StatusBadge), and the Batches filter's words.
Open option BOn a busy day

Option C · The projection drawn

The countdown bar becomes the batch's own projection: the green part is the days its packs take to sell, the hatched end is the last week retailers will not take it. The line says Sells out 6 days before retailers stop. Failed gates are neutral, as in A.

Trade-offs
  • Shows the margin at a glance, and it reads the same on a phone.
  • A new chart to learn: the bar no longer means shelf life left, as it does on every other row and screen.
  • Short margins (a day or two) are hard to see in a 150 px bar.
What the build touches
As A, plus a sell-through bar in the kit (with its accessible name) and core.
Open option COn a busy day
