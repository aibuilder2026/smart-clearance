The ExpireSoon card

SC-151 · Munchly's workspace · three options for the Lister · ExpireSoon card on a batch's Execution, which today shows the marketplace integration as the Lister's raw POST /v1/listings and its 201 Created. Every company, person and figure is fictional.

The maintainer (10 Oct):

“Expire Soon UI work, currently showing HTTP API call, can we improve this marketplace integration piece”

Now

Priya reads JSON to learn what was listed, at what price, for whom and where it stands. The body scrolls sideways at 340 px (the seller's permission, the hidden pincodes and the gs:// path are cut off), the reserve and the territory guard read as two numbers among eleven, and the only state is the badge and a "status" line. The example is the chips (MF-2409-117) once Agrawal Wholesale has won the lot at the ₹14.20 counter.

In every option

The call stays, closed

The request and response stay one tap away, behind The API call (a button with aria-expanded, 44 px), for the audit and for anyone showing the integration. Closed by default.

Five states, from the case

Queued (approved, not posted), live, countered, awarded and collected (the truck loaded). Every figure is the plan's and money.js's: 772 packets at ₹15, reserve ₹13.50, the ₹14.20 counter, the ₹1,644 token.

What does not change

The Negotiator's card and its chat, Open on ExpireSoon (the buyer's view in a sheet), the "not in this plan" card, and the line that the marketplace is mocked.

Option A · The lot as listed

Recommended

The card shows the lot as buyers see it on ExpireSoon (the pack, the price against MRP, best before, the seller, the listing id), then what only Munchly sees (the reserve and the territories it is hidden in), then where the lot stands in four steps: Listed, Bid, Won, Collected, each with its time. Once won, the buyer and the price sit inside the listing.

Trade-offs
  • Reads like the marketplace it is: what was posted is recognisable at a glance, in ExpireSoon's violet.
  • The private terms are named as private, so the reserve can never be mistaken for the price.
  • The steps repeat a little of the Negotiator's card (the bid and the win), at a glance only.
  • The tallest card in idle states.
What the build touches
design3's brand.jsx Execution and screens.css (the listing box, the private rows, the steps, the disclosure); core's Execution.svelte and screens.css verbatim; the workspace's goldens of Execution; the a11y spec scans the disclosure open. No contract or backend change: every field is in the case already.

Option B · The handshake

The integration itself: the two ends (the Lister and ExpireSoon, in sync with the last message's time), then each message between them in plain words, sent or received: listed, a bid, the counter, accepted, collected. Each message opens its own payload.

Trade-offs
  • Shows the marketplace integration best: every call is there, with its direction and time, for a judge or an auditor.
  • Grows with the journey and duplicates the Negotiator's chat almost line for line.
  • Priya learns the price and the terms from a sentence, not a figure.
What the build touches
As A, plus the messages' payloads drawn from the case's bids and award (the webhook bodies are illustrative).

Option C · The terms

The least new design: one line saying where the lot stands, then the terms as a list like every other card on Execution: the lot, asking, reserve, sold by, hidden in, best before, and the label photo.

Trade-offs
  • Quiet and consistent with the kit's lists; the smallest build.
  • Loses the marketplace's look: nothing says this is a listing buyers see.
  • The reserve sits in the same list as the asking price, marked only by its note.
What the build touches
As A, with the kit's List rows and no new component.

Recommended: A. The card is the Lister's work, and its work is a listing: show the listing as buyers see it, mark what they never see, and say where it stands. B is the better integration demo but repeats the Negotiator's card; C is tidy but could be any card.
