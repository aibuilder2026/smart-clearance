The page as it is

The three ways into the demo today, each opening the guided demo's prototype in another tab.

Shared by all three

  • The player is YouTube's, on its no-cookie host, and nothing of YouTube loads until Watch is pressed: no script, no thumbnail, no cookie. YouTube's IFrame API, loaded then, tells the page the film's time (to mark the chapter) and its end. YouTube streams it at the reader's speed, 144p to 1080p. On this board a 30 s cut of the film stands in (marked on the player), until the upload's id is in.
  • The poster is the film's first half-second, the bay at dawn, before the title card that names the client. A square of it is the pill's thumbnail.
  • Twelve chapters, worked out from the film's own timeline (transcript.py): a click plays from one. The same list goes in the YouTube description, so YouTube's own player shows them too (youtube-description.txt, to paste on upload).
  • The film in words: its sound is music, and its 40 captions say what happens, so "Read the film instead" opens them by chapter (WCAG 1.2). They are read only when asked for, so the page's own text never names the client.
  • Where it ends: "One batch, start to finish." with Try the guided demo (to play each part), Book a demo and Watch again. The guided demo will be the live one, smartclearance-demo.web.app, where the page links the Claude Design prototype today.
  • The label is "Watch the 5-minute demo" everywhere: the film runs 4:56.

The three options

What differs is where the film plays, and so what Watch does from each of its three places.

A · The theatre

Recommendeda dialogevery Watch the same

Every Watch, in the hero, the pill or the close, opens the film in a dark room over the page, as large as the window allows (up to 1280 px wide, never taller than the window). The page stays where it was, its own film holds, and Escape, the close button or a click outside put focus back on the Watch that opened it. Under the film: its twelve chapters as chips, the one playing marked, a click to jump; and the film in words. On a phone the room is the whole screen.

Why A. It answers all three ways in at once, from anywhere on the page, with no scroll and no change to the page's layout: the reader stays where they were and comes back to it. It is the pattern a visitor expects from "Watch", and the kit's modal rules (focus kept in, returned, Escape) carry over. Trade-off: the film is not seen until someone presses Watch; nothing on the page shows the chapters before then. The build: a DemoFilm dialog in frontend/admin (core's focus trap, counting the player's iframe), the three Watch links turned to buttons, the hero's film held while it is open.

B · A section of its own

Spoiler Alert'sa new section after the statement

A new section after the statement, "See one batch, start to finish.": the poster large with its play button and running time, the twelve chapters beside it (under it, as chips, on a phone and a tablet), and the film in words. It plays in place. The three Watch links take the reader to it and start it; the pill keeps clear of it, as it does of the workspace section.

Why B. The film is on the page for everyone who scrolls, not only those who press Watch, and the chapter list tells the product's story even unplayed, as Spoiler Alert's section does. Trade-off: a longer page; Watch from the close or the pill scrolls the reader back up the page to play; playing in place is smaller than A's room. The build: a Film section between Statement and Scene, its CSS in site.css; the Watch links become links to #film that start it.

C · In the hero

the first screenthe hero's own grammar

Watch makes the first screen the film's: the heading and the strip step aside, the film plays full in the hero's frame under the bar, a line over it names the chapter it is in, and the strip under it becomes the film's twelve chapters, lit as it goes, like the hero strip's four. "Back to the page" or Escape gives the hero back and returns focus. The pill and the close scroll to the top and start it there.

Why C. The most cinematic: the film is where the reader's eyes already are, and the hero's strip grammar carries over to the film's chapters. Trade-off: Watch from the pill or the close jumps the reader to the top; the hero's own film and copy are hidden while it plays; on a phone the first screen is tall and the film is letterboxed in it. The build: the hero gains a playing state (Hero.svelte), the bar takes its glass while it plays, and the chapter strip reads the film's time from YouTube's player.
