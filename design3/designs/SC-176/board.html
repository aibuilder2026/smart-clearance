The journey clock explained

SC-176 · Munchly's workspace, live · three ways to say, wherever the workspace shows the journey's date and time, that it is the prototype's own clock, and to show how it is started again and moved forward.

The maintainer (11 Oct), with a screenshot of the Command Center's “Live · Fri 2 Oct 08:00”:

“Wherever on UI we see date and time I want an info note to be added that this was done as part of the prototype to be able to run it with defined dataset of SKU with best before dates. And show them steps how journey can be reset fast forwarded.”

Why the workspace has its own clock

A fixed dataset

Munchly's nine batches of eight SKUs each carry a best-before date (MF-2409-117, the Masala Chips: 18 Nov 2026). A synthetic workspace replays the story's own calendar, from Fri 2 Oct 2026, day 0, so the gates, the sell-through and every figure come out as the story has them (SC-73, SC-81).

Days can be short

A client's journey day lasts 1 to 1,440 minutes (SC-66): Real time, Rehearsal (an hour), Demo (five minutes) or Fast (a minute), set in the console's badge (SC-68).

Staff move it

In the console, Reset journey puts the journey back to day 0 and keeps the client's setup (SC-79, SC-88); the Agents tab fires a scheduled run or timer now: Run now, Close now, Report now (SC-79, SC-94).

What the workspace shows today

In every option

One guide, “Prototype time”

Why the date is the story's, the journey time now and its pace, then Start the journey again (Clients → Munchly Foods → ⋯ → Reset journey…, a day length, Reset journey) and Move it forward (the badge's shorter days; the Agents tab's Run now, Close now, Report now), and setting the badge back to Real time afterwards. Open the console under it. At the side on a desktop, from the bottom on a phone.

Wherever the clock is

Every page's title and a batch's head in the workspace, for every role, and the bar's short line once a title collapses. In the console, the same note beside Munchly's journey-day badge, where the controls are. Only the live workspace has the clock; the stub and the guided demo don't change.

Nothing else moves

The clock still steps a quarter hour at a time and is never announced (WCAG 2.2.2, SC-68). The note is a button or a link, focusable, with a 44 px target.

The options

Option A · At the clock

Recommended

The clock gains one more piece after the date, the time and the pace: a small blue ⓘ Prototype time button. It opens the guide. Under the bar's collapsed title it is the icon alone. On a phone it wraps to its own row under the clock.

Trade-offs
  • Says “prototype” right where the date is, on every page, at the cost of one pill.
  • The steps stay out of the way until someone asks.
  • The other dates on a page (the tracker's stops, the feed, best-before) are not marked; they all run on this clock.
Open option AA batch's page

Option B · A note under the clock

Under the clock, a line of its own: “Prototype time: the story's own calendar, so its batches' best-before dates hold.” and a link, How to reset or fast-forward, which opens the guide. Always in view, never behind an icon. Under the bar's collapsed title the time keeps an info button.

Trade-offs
  • Nobody misses it: the reason is read without a tap.
  • A second line under every title, read again on every page, for as long as the prototype runs.
  • Like A, the other dates are not marked.
Open option BA batch's page

Option C · Every date marked

Every date and time of the journey on the page is underlined with dots: the clock, the tracker's stops, the agents' feed, a batch's best-before. Pointing at one or focusing it says “Prototype time · press for how to reset or fast-forward”, and pressing it opens the guide. The clock also gains a small Prototype tag.

Trade-offs
  • The most literal answer to “wherever we see date and time”.
  • Dozens of dotted underlines on a busy page; they read as links or as warnings, and a tooltip doesn't reach a phone.
  • The build touches every component that prints a date (the tracker, the feeds, the papers, the timelines), not one line.

Recommended: option A

At the clock puts the note where every date on the page comes from: the journey clock under the title. It says “prototype” on every page without adding a line to read, and the guide carries the full steps for whoever wants to reset or fast-forward. Marking every date (C) answers the words of the request but clutters every screen; a sentence on every page (B) is read once and then skipped.
