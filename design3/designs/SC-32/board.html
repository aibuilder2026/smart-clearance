Option 1 · Take the wheel

The tour plays, then the picture is the visitor's to drag, pinch and open

The camera follows the batch through the whole business, then hands the picture over:

  • drag or swipe to look round, pinch or Ctrl-scroll to zoom;
  • tap any place or agent for what happened there and what it did;
  • "The whole business" takes the camera back out.
Open the mockupIn dark
Why it works
The plainest way to give you all four asks: one picture of the business, every agent at its post, the tour, and a picture you can explore on any screen. It is light: an image and a few hundred lines of interface.
Trade-off
Its depth is the plate's own. Next to option 2 it can feel flat when the camera dives.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: the hero's picture becomes a stage with a camera; the places, the agents' posts, the graph and the panel lie on it; the new plates go in;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same, with motion for the camera.

Option 2 · In depth

The same, with the picture given depth: it parallaxes, tilts and focuses like a camera in a model

Recommended

Option 1, drawn in WebGL2 with the plate's depth map:

  • as the camera travels, near things move more than far ones, and they grow faster as it nears;
  • the pointer tilts the model a little, and a swipe sways it on phones;
  • the focus follows the camera: what is nearer or farther than the place goes soft, like a tilt-shift lens racking focus;
  • the agents and the pins ride the same depth, so they stay on their places.
Open the mockupIn dark
Why recommended
It is the wow you asked for. The whole business reads as a real miniature you are moving through, not a photo being zoomed, and it keeps every bit of option 1's interaction.
Trade-off
It is the heaviest of the three: a small WebGL2 renderer (about 150 lines) and a 20 kB depth map. Where WebGL2 is missing it falls back to option 1. It draws only while something moves, and under reduced motion there is no tilt or sway.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: option 1's stage, plus the depth renderer and the depth map;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same, with a WebGL2 canvas behind the stage's layers.

Option 3 · Your yes

The tour stops at the plan and waits for the visitor's own yes before the batch sells

The camera follows the batch to the maker's office and stops there:

  • the plan is on screen: 588 packs to 31 kiranas, 772 to one buyer, ₹21,770 back instead of −₹26,330;
  • "Approve the plan" waits, amber, at the office;
  • the visitor's yes sends the batch out to the kiranas and up the highway, and the money comes back;
  • then the picture is theirs to explore, as in option 1.
Open the mockupIn dark
Why it works
"You say yes once" becomes something the visitor does, and that sticks.
Trade-off
Visitors who never press it don't see the sale play out. The caption tells them the result anyway, and the steps go past it.
The build would touch
  • design3/site: option 1's stage, plus the yes at the office;
  • the SvelteKit port, the same.

Decide

Pick one option for the hero, or ask for another round. Parts can move between options before the build: option 3's yes inside option 2's picture in depth, for example.
