What happens today in the product

Under Munchly's full credit policy the packs left at the godown come back to Munchly, which destroys them; the expiry credit note carries no GST, Munchly's destruction certificate counts them and its GST memo reverses its own input credit. The gap: the distributor keeps the input credit he took on packs he no longer has, and no paper deals with it. The example below is the leftover chips case: 444 of the scheme's 588 packets ordered, so 144 packs are left at Kalamna Market godown.

Route B, in every option

The money

A financial credit note (no GST charged or adjusted) from Munchly to Rakesh Traders: 144 × ₹22 = ₹3,168.00, plus the ₹158.40 of input GST he reverses on them (grossed up, so he ends whole), plus the agency's ₹216.00 destruction charges. ₹3,542.40 in all, adjusted against his account.

The GST

The packs were Rakesh Traders' stock, bought under Munchly's tax invoice: he destroys them, so section 17(5)(h) falls on his credit, reversed in his GSTR-3B (Table 4(B)(1)). Munchly destroyed nothing: its output tax on the sale stands and it keeps its own input credit (₹1,224, ₹0.90 a pack). No EPR charge on destroying them (EPR, point 5, is a later story).

The destruction, as it is done today

The packets are taken to the authorised municipal landfill, slit open, tipped into a pit and covered by a JCB, with a slate in the frame carrying the batch, the count and the date. The agency, the corporation's authorised waste contractor, issues its certificate. The batch cannot close until Supply Chain approves the evidence.

The papers, after Priya's yes

The agency's destruction certificate (for Rakesh Traders: the batch, the packs and kilos, the method, the agency's authorisation, the evidence and who approved it, and his GST reversal), Munchly's expiry credit note (financial, against that certificate) and the GST ITC memo (credit kept). Each has its PDF; Rakesh's copies carry the certificate and the credit note.

The console: the client's expiry policy

The supply-chain profile's Expiry policy gains Destroyed at the distributor's godown, and Munchly is set to it. Under it: the evidence asked for, the reviewer (the batch closes on that yes), the authorised agencies, what the credit covers, and when the distributor is asked again. Taken back by Munchly becomes the GST-correct route A (a GST credit note against Munchly's invoice), offered but not built in this round.

The options: how the evidence comes in and how Priya approves it

Option A · On Execution

Rakesh's Today asks for one photo of the packs destroyed, and who destroyed them. Priya reviews it in a card on the batch's Execution, after Left at the godown: the photo, the packs, the agency, and Approve · issue the papers or Ask again.

Trade-offs
  • The smallest change: one photo, one card.
  • One photo cannot show both the batch and its destruction; nothing checks it but Priya.
  • The approval sits on Execution, so it waits until Priya opens that batch.

Option B · A second yes

Recommended

Like the label photo and the plan's approval. Rakesh sends two photos (before, at the godown with the batch label; after, at the landfill with the slate) and the agency's certificate number. Vision checks them: the batch number, the count against the 144 left, when and where, the slate. Priya gets 1 waiting for your yes on the Command Center and a push, and approves in a sheet that shows both photos, Vision's checks and exactly what her yes issues.

Trade-offs
  • The evidence real brands ask for: the batch before, the destruction after, the slate, the agency's certificate.
  • Vision does the checking a person would, so Priya's yes is quick and safe; Ask again says why.
  • Mirrors the plan's approval, which the workspace already teaches.
  • The most to build: a Vision check of the photos, and a second approval flow.
What the build touches
As A, plus Vision's destruction check (the agents' Vision pipeline, a new eval set), the Command Center's waiting count and the approve sheet's second kind.

Option C · Witnessed

Rakesh books a slot with an authorised agency, and Munchly's area sales manager (Sunil Patil, fictional) witnesses the destruction and takes the photos in the app; the agency's certificate is uploaded; Priya approves after the witness.

Trade-offs
  • The strongest control: a Munchly person at every destruction.
  • Slow and costly for 144 packs; most brands keep witnessing for large or high-value lots.
  • A new person in the workspace (the area sales manager) and a booking step.
What the build touches
As A, plus a new role and member, a booking and witness flow, and the console's witness setting.

Option B, A second yes.
