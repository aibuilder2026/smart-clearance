What the labels show today

Five lines: the product, the batch, the made and best-before dates, the MRP. No barcode, no QR code, no FSSAI licence, no maker's address. Every photo Munchly's workspace holds reads this way: the 12 batches cleared since July and the Masala Chips. The Mango Drink has no photo of its own yet.

In every option

Codes that scan

Every code is drawn to its standard and read back from the finished photo with a barcode reader before it ships. A GS1 EAN-13 for the pack (Masala Chips 8900000001019) or an ITF-14 for the carton of 24 (18900000001016), and a QR code that is the batch's GS1 Digital Link: id.munchly.example/01/18900000001016/10/MF-2409-117?17=261118, the product, the batch and its best-before.

FSSAI and GS1, as they are

FSSAI does not issue barcodes: it asks for its logo and the maker's 14-digit licence number on the label (Munchly's 10019022001234), and Legal Metrology for the maker's address, net quantity, MRP incl. of all taxes and customer care. The barcode is GS1's. Every number is fictional: the GTINs sit under GS1 India's 890 with a company number no business holds, the QR on a reserved .example domain, and the fssai mark is drawn here, not the authority's artwork.

Vision still reads it

The batch, made date, best-before and MRP print exactly as each batch's record has them, so Vision's label check still matches (the chips: 18/05/2026, 18/11/2026, ₹30). Each photo keeps today's carton and light: the label is printed onto a Qwen base photo as ink, not pasted on.

The options

Option A · The full statutory sticker

Recommended

A printed sticker like the reference: the product in a band with the veg mark, the ingredients, the net quantity and MRP boxed, the batch and its dates boxed, the maker and customer care, the FSSAI licence, the pack's EAN-13 and the batch's QR code, and the storage line.

Trade-offs
  • Reads as the label the maintainer sent: every field a pack's label carries.
  • The batch and its dates stay large, in their own box, so Vision reads them as easily as today.
  • The busiest of the three: the most for Vision to look past.

Option B · The printed shipper carton

No sticker: the carton itself, printed in one colour at the factory (the brand, the product, 24 × 150 g, handling marks, the FSSAI licence, the maker), with the carton's ITF-14 and the batch's QR code, and the batch, its dates and MRP inkjet-coded into a box on the line.

Trade-offs
  • How most shipper cartons in a godown actually look: an ITF-14 is the code a carton carries.
  • The inkjet dot type is harder to read than a sticker, a more honest test of Vision.
  • No ingredients (they are on each pack inside), and it looks least like the reference.

Option C · Today's label with a coded foot

Today's five lines as they are, with a foot added: the pack's EAN-13, the FSSAI licence with the maker, and the batch's QR code.

Trade-offs
  • Changes least: Vision reads the same five lines it reads now.
  • Still no ingredients, net quantity or customer care: less like the reference.

What the build changes, whichever is picked

Every Munchly batch

A photo for each batch in the workspace's history and journey: the 12 cleared since July, the Masala Chips and the Mango Drink (new). The three destruction photos taken at the godown (SC-139) show the carton's label too, and are redrawn with it.

Wherever a photo is sent

The history's photos (system/img/labels/, backend-api's reference copies), the photos the journey suites and walk.sh upload, the label the stub draws, and then the local workspace's photos uploaded again. Production only on your yes.

Vision's eval set

The two story photos in it become the new labels. The other 71 eval photos test hard reads (blur, glare, other SKUs) and stay as they are, unless you say otherwise. A live eval of the new photos runs only on request (about GBP 0.10).
