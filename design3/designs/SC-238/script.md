## The film in one line

A batch of mango drink has twenty-two days left and no app will take it. In one morning, ten agents and one yes find
every pack a home.

- **Dramatic question:** will this batch reach people before its date, or end in the bin?
- **Visual thesis:** the batch's clock. It opens cold, still and alone (one carton in a sea of cartons, steel-blue
  dawn through roof slits) and ends warm, connected and moving (many hands, many places, evening practicals).
- **Colour script:** steel-blue dawn and a red write-off (the problem) → Smart-Clearance green, glass and light (the
  platform and the agents) → warm morning gold, kirana tungsten, a sunlit dock (the journey) → overcast grey-green
  and one amber light (the hardest case) → indigo evening with warm practicals and the ledger's green (the record).
- **Register:** drama in the bookends, a confident product film in the middle. One narrator (a warm Indian woman,
  the films' storyteller) carries the guide; the people speak only where a person would.

## How it is shot

- **Dialogue as a short film, not testimonials.** Nobody speaks to the lens. One speaker a clip, eyeline off-camera to
  a partner, medium close-ups with foreground, shallow depth, motivated light (a window, a tube light, a practical).
  Lines of six words or fewer on camera; anything longer is the narrator. Exchanges are built as shot and reverse
  shot, the overlap made in the edit with J and L cuts.
- **The phone call is the spine.** The film opens and closes on the distributor and the lead on one phone call each,
  cross-cut between two places: at dawn it ends in "Write it off, then."; in the evening, weeks later, it ends in
  "And nothing written off."
- **Every frame moves.** No still photograph is a scene. Behind every app screen plays the footage of the moment it
  belongs to, defocused under the brand's green; every graphic animates in, holds while it is read, and leaves.
- **The app's screens are the app's, pixel-sharp.** Captured from the product (the production recordings and the
  workspace's own build), never upscaled past their pixels; on a monitor or a phone in a shot, the real screen is
  tracked onto the glass, and its pop-out is the same screen, its pointer landing on what it names.
- **Papers lie flat and square.** Each paper is shown straight-on at full sharpness, the camera gliding down it while
  the voice names its lines, over the footage of what it records.
- **Transitions are motivated, never mush:** cut on action, cut on look, cut on sound (J-cuts), match cuts on one
  carrier, a whip with directional blur in one direction only, the foreground becoming the next scene. No dissolve
  between two faces, no blur on a resting screen.
- **Sound everywhere:** one continuous room tone and ambience bed per place (never re-triggered at a cut), foley on
  every visible action, a clean UI sound on every real tap and arrival, a soft whoosh only on real transitions, and the
  score (SC-210's cues in D, reused) under it all, ducked under every word.

## The script (revision 1, after the storyboard critic)

Revision 1 takes every point of `review/r0-storyboard.md`: the morning follows the product's own clock (the 09:00 flag,
the 09:16 photo, the 09:20 plan, the 09:35 yes, the 09:36 offer, the next day's sale and pickup, expiry day's ledger),
every journey scene carries its clock chip; nothing is claimed that is mocked (no integrations beat; "reviewed", not
"filed"; "thirty per cent"; "in Hindi"); the bin is "if written off"; Act III counts nine stops and ten agents with an
amber gate for the person, gives each stop at least 4 s, dims the Lister and Negotiator on the mango ("too close to its
date"), runs over moving footage graded green and gives each stop its real screen on a stem; Act IV shows the agents as
heads (name · stop · model) instead of explaining them again; Act V opens on the chips' split with the lot first; every
device is raised with its back to the camera and its screen seen full-frame; the call to action is held 5 s after the
last word. One call stays the director's: nobody speaks to the lens (SC-211 asked for the camera; this brief asks for a
short film), named in the review for the maintainer.
