The options

Option A · The same sum on both pages

Recommended

Batches and Orders open with one card that reads the same on both: what the cleared batches cost him = what he sold + what Munchly credited him. The page's own part is marked “this page”; the other links to its page; a bar shows the split. Every batch carries its own sum the same way: on Batches its credit leads (“₹7,821 credited + ₹14,640 sold = ₹22,461”), on Orders its sales.

Trade-offs
  • Nothing to open: the sum is on the page, the first thing read.
  • Each row answers “why is this number different from the other page” on its own line.
  • A bigger card at the top of both pages.
Open option ABatches

Option B · How this adds up, on request

Each page keeps its own figure as today (Batches: credited; Orders: sold). Its card gains How this adds up, which opens one sheet, the same from both pages: every cleared batch with what it cost him, what he sold, what was credited and how he ended, and the totals. The two pages' figures are two columns of one table.

Trade-offs
  • The pages change least.
  • The whole working in one table, batch by batch.
  • It takes a tap to find, and the rows still show different figures on the two pages.
Open option BOrders

Option C · One figure on both pages

Both pages lead with the same figure: what came back to him from the batches he cleared, all they cost him (₹1,72,846), then where it came from as a bar and two parts, sold (Orders) and credited (Batches). Every batch reads the same on both pages: “₹22,461 back”, its split, and its two parts.

Trade-offs
  • The pages agree outright: the same figure at the top and on every row.
  • Leads with what he got back, the figure a distributor cares about.
  • Neither page leads with its own subject any more: Orders' sales and Batches' credit become the smaller line.
