Munchly's workspace, live

SC-68 · munchly.smartclearance.com · epic SC-66, Workspace v1. Three options for what a live backend adds to Munchly's workspace, each built on the app itself (its shell, screens, kit and stylesheets) with the motion it would ship. Every company, person and figure is fictional, and every figure is computed by core/money.js.

The maintainer's decisions for Workspace v1:

  • Sign-in: email and password only, the same approach as the console. Phone with a one-time code and Google sign-in go away. Munchly's people use @munchly.example; everyone else (distributors, kiranas, the Raipur buyer, food banks) uses @google.example. Accounts are made by invitation on a default password; no email is ever sent, so there is no forgot-password flow. One message covers any wrong sign-in, because email enumeration protection is on.
  • Live data: updates over SSE, push notifications through FCM, and a journey clock that can be compressed. A client setting makes one journey day last 1 to 1,440 minutes, and screens show journey time.
  • Partners act only by signing in themselves; there are no stand-ins.

The workspace now

Today the sign-in asks for an email or a mobile number, then Google or a one-time code, and the account chips and Explore as someone in the story step into anyone. The app assumes one hero batch: the tracker card, the Route Room and the Batches list all open MF-2409-117. Nothing says whether the screen is current, and every time is the story's fixed time.

What every option shares

The sign-in

  • The console's form (SC-46, picked A) in Munchly's own card: Munchly leads, “Powered by Smart-Clearance” at the foot, the product's hero beside it on desktops (SC-24).
  • Email, password with a 44 × 40 show or hide, Sign in. autocomplete username and current-password, so password managers fill both.
  • One message for any wrong sign-in, in red above the button, read out, the password cleared. No forgot-password: “Whoever invited you gives you your first password. Nothing is sent by email.”
  • Signing in keeps the button's words: “Signing in…” with the mark's S drawing, then “Welcome, Priya” (SC-49).

Signed in, not a member

  • The password was right, so it is safe to say so: “Signed in as Neha Kulkarni”, and that the account isn't a member of Munchly Foods' workspace.
  • Sign in with another account, or find your workspace. B also lists where the account does sign in.
  • The example is a Smart-Clearance staff account, which shares the one Firebase pool.

No stand-ins

  • “Explore as someone in the story”, the account chips, Switch person and every “Continue as Rakesh bhai” go.
  • A batch waiting on a partner says who and how: “Waiting for Lakshmi Agencies' photo. She sends it from her own phone.”

A batch's own Route Room

  • /route/MF-2410-118: the Command Center, the Batches list and notifications open the batch's own room.
  • The mockups flag a second batch this morning, Mango Drink MF-2410-118 at Lakshmi Agencies: 22 days left, 1,580 of 2,000 units at risk, −₹22,657 if destroyed, waiting at Verify for its label photo.

When something fails

  • Approve, failed: the sheet stays open and says so: “The approval didn't go through. Smart-Clearance didn't answer in time, so nothing was listed, offered or sent. The plan is still waiting for you.” Retry stays amber: it is still the human yes.
  • Offline, Approve is unavailable with its reason beside it, never silently disabled.

Access and motion

  • WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes: 4.5:1 on the real ground, 44 px targets, visible focus. The reconnect and offline marks are grey, never red or amber; red stays risk, amber the yes.
  • The clock steps a quarter hour at a time (every 3.1 s at 1 day = 5 min), changes in place and is never a live region. Only loading indicators turn: the reconnect loader and the first-load wash. Back live, the dot pings twice. Under reduced motion everything lands at once.

The sign-in, in every option

Option A · In the shell

Recommended

Everything live sits in one quiet place every role already has: the journey clock and the connection at the foot of the sidebar, a chip in the bar on phones. The approved screens keep their layout, and the batch in focus is named where the Route Room already names its batch.

The clock
Fri 2 Oct · 09:31, Journey time and 1 day = 5 min, in a block at the foot of the sidebar; on phones a chip by the bell, 09:31 with the pace glyph. Either opens a sheet: the date in full, the pace (“A 47-day batch journey takes about 3.9 hours”) and whether updates are arriving.
The connection
The same block or chip: a still green dot when live; a turning loader with Reconnecting · updates paused at 09:31; the wifi-off glyph with Offline · showing 09:31. The tracker card greys its tracker and says when it paused; Approve is unavailable offline, with the reason beside it.
The first load
The shell draws at once and the screen stands as its own shape (SC-49's placeholders, one green wash), the chip saying Catching up; the content rises into place.
The push ask
A card at the top of the person's home after the first sign-in, never a modal and never over a task; Not now moves it to Profile. On an iPhone outside the Home Screen it shows the three install steps; when blocked, how to allow it again.
Several flagged
The batch that needs a person leads as the tracker card; the rest sit under it as rows with their stop. In the Route Room the batch's name is a switcher: the flagged batches in a menu (the WAI-ARIA menu-button pattern), previous and next beside it, All batches at the foot.
Uploads
Rakesh bhai's photo shows its progress on the photo itself, as the messaging apps he uses do, with Cancel in the ring; Setup's export has a progress row and the three steps the file goes through.
Trade-offs
The sidebar's foot is peripheral: someone mid-approval may not see Reconnecting there, so the tracker card says it too. The menu hides the second batch behind one click; the Command Center's rows keep it in view.
What the build touches
  • design3: K.Shell's footer, Screen's actions, the Command Center and Route Room for a batch in the address, the approve sheet, the camera, Setup.
  • frontend: Shell.svelte, Screen.svelte, a live store fed by SSE, /route/[ref].

Option B · In the header, batch by batch

Every page says under its title whether it is live and what journey time it is; a dropped stream becomes a band across the page. The batches flagged this morning are tabs. Signing in runs into the console's splash, and the first sign-in ends on one step that asks for notifications before the home opens.

The clock
A line under every large title: Live · Fri 2 Oct 09:31 · 1 day = 5 min. When the title collapses into the bar, a short version sits under the bar's title.
The connection
The line's first word changes, and a band sticks under the bar while it lasts: Reconnecting… Updates paused at 09:31, so what you see may be behind; anything you do still goes through. Offline adds Try again.
The first load
The console's splash (SC-51, option A) in Munchly's workspace: the reads as stops with the time each took, the greeting by the journey's hour, and the mark opening onto the page.
The push ask
One step between the first sign-in and the home: a lock-screen preview of the very push it is asking for (the 09:23 plan, with the money), then Turn on notifications or Not now. The iPhone variant draws Safari's share sheet; the blocked one shows the preview greyed and the steps.
Several flagged
Tabs over the Command Center's tracker card, one a batch with its stop (amber when it waits on your yes), and the same tabs under the Route Room's title. All flagged batches stay in view; on phones the tabs scroll sideways.
Uploads
The Send button fills as the photo goes, its words readable over both halves; Setup's export as in A.
Trade-offs
The line under the title scrolls away with it; the band and the bar's short line carry it after that. A first-run step delays the home by one screen, once. Tabs suit two to five flagged batches; beyond that they scroll.

  • design3: Screen's header and a band slot, the tabs, the console's splash in the app, a first-run step in RoleApp.

Option C · On the tracker

The tracker leaves the card and docks at the foot of every screen, the way a delivery app keeps a live order in view: the batch in focus, its stop, journey time and the pace, whether updates arrive, and a pager across the flagged batches. Whatever is in motion uses the same bar.

The clock
In the bar, Fri 2 Oct 09:31 · 1 day = 5 min beside the batch, and in the tracker card's head: the journey's time is told where the batch's time is.
The connection
The bar's segments grey and its words say reconnecting · paused at 09:31 or offline · as of 09:31; the tracker card says the same.
The first load
The screen's shape, and the bar: Catching up with the agents, this morning's batches in, live updates next.
The push ask
Docked in the bar's place after the first sign-in, so it never covers a task; dismissed, the bar returns to the batch.
Several flagged
The Command Center's tracker cards are a deck with a pager; the bar follows the card in front, and in a Route Room pages from one batch's room to the next. On the Route Room the bar takes over the approve bar: Review and approve sits in it.
Failures and uploads
A failed approval stays in the bar after the sheet closes, with Retry. Uploads go on in the bar, so Rakesh bhai can leave the camera while the photo sends.
Trade-offs
The strongest idea and the most chrome: on a 390 phone the bar takes about 70 px above the tab bar, so pages need room under their last item and focus must never sit behind it (WCAG 2.4.11). Toasts move above it. It is a new component on every screen.
What the build touches
  • design3: a docked bar in RoleApp, the Route Room's approve bar merged into it, the deck.
  • frontend: a LiveBar.svelte in every app layout, scroll padding, a focus store shared by the deck and the bar.

Recommended: A, in the shell. The clock and the connection are app-wide facts, so they belong to the one place every role already has on every screen, Priya's Command Center and Ganesh ji's Offers alike, and the shell carries them without adding anything over a task. It changes the approved screens the least: the tracker card stays the hero, the push ask is a card that waits on the home, and the batch in focus is named where the Route Room already names its batch. It is also the smallest build on the live backend. B is the better choice if the maintainer wants several flagged batches in view at all times; C is the boldest and most on-brand, at the price of chrome on every phone screen.

Open questions

  • Judges' accounts. With real passwords, “Explore as someone in the story” and the account chips go. Do judges get a printed list of the demo accounts with the default password, or should the sign-in keep chips that fill the email only?
  • The workspace admin's Users screen still invites by “Email or mobile number”. Should it follow the console's email-only invitation (the console board, SC-68 console review.html, in platform v3)?
  • The Raipur buyer now signs in at munchly.smartclearance.com with a google.example address. He keeps ExpireSoon's own look, as today?
  • The clock and WCAG 2.2.2. The journey clock updates on its own beside the content. The options treat it as essential (every figure is read in journey time), step it a quarter hour at a time and never announce it. Agreed, or should a person be able to pause it?
