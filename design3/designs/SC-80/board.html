The label photo: take one, or upload one

SC-80 · Munchly's workspace · three options for the distributor's label photo step, where Vision asks for one carton label before anything is priced. Every company, person and figure is fictional.

The request, from the maintainer (8 Oct), looking at the step on a laptop:

  • “This should be modified… 1. Either take photo. 2. Upload an already taken photo.”

The step now

A drawing of the label in a viewfinder, a shutter, and a 30 px gallery icon with no words. On the live workspace the shutter opens the phone's camera on a touch screen and the file picker on a laptop, and nothing on the screen says either. On a laptop the frame looks like a camera that is on.

What all three share

Both ways, in words

  • Take a photo and Upload a photo are both on the screen, labelled, each at least 44 px.
  • On a phone, taking a photo opens the phone's camera. Uploading opens its gallery, or the files on a laptop.
  • On a laptop, a photo can also be dropped onto the frame or the upload choice. Dropping always has a button beside it.

What is sent

  • What backend-api takes: JPEG, PNG or WebP, under 8 MB, said once under the choices.
  • Naming those types makes an iPhone hand over its HEIC photos as JPEG.
  • A photo in hand is shown whole, in its own shape, so the batch, dates and MRP can be checked before it goes.

After Send, unchanged

  • Send fills as the photo goes (SC-73). Vision then reads the label, and the step ends on the verified record.
  • The guided demo and the stub keep a simulated photo; only the demo's hint follows the new words.
  • Motion: the photo fades in (240 ms) and the buttons swap (160 ms), on the system's ease. Under reduced motion both land at once.

Option A · Two ways under the frame

Recommended

The frame stays as the guide, now marked Example and captioned “Like this: one carton label, close up”. Under it are two large buttons, Take a photo and Upload a photo. The primary follows the device: Take on a phone, Upload on a laptop. On a laptop, Take a photo opens its camera inside the frame, landscape, with the shutter, Cancel and Upload under it.

Where
The bar under the frame, and the frame's own words.
Trade-offs
  • The closest to the approved screen: the same frame and hint, and both ways named in the same place on every device.
  • Nothing asks for the camera until he chooses to take a photo.
  • The frame is an example until he does. The Example chip and the hint say so, but it is still a picture where a camera might be expected.
What the build touches
  • design3: screens/trade.jsx (CameraInner) and screens/screens.css.
  • The frontend: CameraInner.svelte and core's screens.css, which is verbatim with a drift test. On a laptop, Take a photo uses the browser's camera (getUserMedia).
  • The demo's hint and two workspace e2e specs, which press “Take the photo”.
  • No contract change.

Option B · Choose first

No viewfinder until he chooses. A card says what Vision needs, with the label as an example. Under it are two choices the size of a list row: Take a photo (“With your camera, at the shelf”) and Upload a photo (“One you already took”). The photo then shows on its own, with a short check of what must be readable before Send photo.

Where
The whole step, in two screens: choose, then check and send.
Trade-offs
  • The plainest: two choices, no camera metaphor to misread.
  • The check before sending asks him to look, which suits a photo Vision may otherwise ask for again.
  • It loses the viewfinder that shows how to frame the label, and the step feels like a form, not a camera.
  • It is the biggest change to a screen the story and the demo show.
What the build touches
As A, with a new layout in CameraInner and the review card.
Open option BCheck, then send

Option C · The real viewfinder

The camera opens in the frame as the step opens: the phone's rear camera, or a laptop's camera in landscape. The shutter takes the photo there. Upload sits beside the shutter with its name under it, where a phone's camera keeps its gallery. If the camera is refused or missing, the frame says so and offers Upload.

Where
The frame itself, and the bar under it.
Trade-offs
  • The most like a camera: what the frame shows is what he will send.
  • The browser asks for the camera as soon as the step opens, a second permission for a distributor who has just given the one-time permission.
  • A photo taken in the browser is lower in quality than one from the phone's own camera app: no flash or HDR control, on cheap Android phones in a dim godown.
  • It needs a state for a refused camera.
What the build touches
As A, with getUserMedia on every device, the refused-camera state, and the stream stopped when he leaves the step.
Open option CThe camera refused

Recommended: A. It answers the request directly, with both ways named under the frame on every device. It keeps the approved screen and its guide to framing the label, and asks for the camera only when he chooses to take a photo. On a phone, taking a photo uses the phone's own camera app, which takes the better photo in a dim godown. B is clearer still but drops the guide. C feels most like a camera, but it asks for the camera up front and takes a poorer photo on the phones the trade uses.

One question

On a laptop, should Take a photo open the laptop's camera (as drawn in A and C), or should laptops offer Upload only? A laptop's camera faces the person, so photographing a carton label with it is awkward. It does work for a label held up to it, as in a demo.
