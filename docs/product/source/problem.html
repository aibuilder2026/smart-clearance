1The problem

A near-expiry batch has one easy exit today: the bin.

Packaged food that quick-commerce will no longer take still has days, sometimes weeks, of life. In India it mostly waits at the distributor's godown, comes back as an expiry claim, and is destroyed. The brand pays for the stock, the disposal, the EPR, and loses the GST input credit on top.

3
It sits, and the date comes closer

Normal sales cannot move it in time. Nobody decides where else it could go, batch by batch.

4
An expiry claim

The distributor sends it back against the brand's return policy, or writes it off himself.

5
Destroyed

Food that could have been sold or eaten goes to landfill, and the loss lands on the brand's books.

Why nobody fixes it today

Demand is scattered

Millions of kiranas, about a lakh of distributors, young clearance marketplaces and licensed food banks. No buyer takes a truckload.

Every exit has its own rules

Shelf-life gates, the territory the brand protects, what each shop can sell in a fortnight, the days a food bank needs.

Days, not weeks

Every day spent deciding is a day off the date. Stock with 22 days left cannot wait for a month-end review.

Every move needs paper

Tax invoices, credit notes, the GST credit kept or reversed, the FSSAI checklist, receipts, a destruction certificate, the BRSR line.
